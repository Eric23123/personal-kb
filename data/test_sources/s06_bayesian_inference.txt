# Bayesian Inference Methods

## Bayes' Theorem

For parameters θ and data D:

    p(θ|D) = p(D|θ) p(θ) / p(D) ∝ p(D|θ) p(θ)

where p(θ|D) is the posterior, p(D|θ) is the likelihood, p(θ) is the prior.

## Conjugate Priors

A prior is conjugate to a likelihood if the posterior belongs to the same family:

- Beta-Binomial: θ ~ Beta(α, β), X|θ ~ Binomial(n, θ) → θ|X ~ Beta(α+X, β+n-X)
- Normal-Normal (known variance): θ ~ N(μ₀, τ₀²), X_i|θ ~ N(θ, σ²) with n observations
  → θ|X ~ N((μ₀/τ₀² + nX̄/σ²)/(1/τ₀² + n/σ²), 1/(1/τ₀² + n/σ²))
- Gamma-Poisson: λ ~ Gamma(α, β), X|λ ~ Poisson(λ) → λ|X ~ Gamma(α+ΣX_i, β+n)

## Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)

When the posterior is not analytically tractable, MCMC methods sample from it:

- Metropolis-Hastings: Propose θ* ~ q(θ*|θ_t), accept with probability
  α = min(1, [p(θ*|D) q(θ_t|θ*)] / [p(θ_t|D) q(θ*|θ_t)])

- Gibbs sampling: Sample each θ_i from its full conditional p(θ_i|θ_{-i}, D)

## Variational Inference

Approximate the posterior p(θ|D) with a simpler distribution q(θ) by minimizing
KL(q || p). The evidence lower bound (ELBO) is:

    ELBO = E_q[log p(D, θ)] - E_q[log q(θ)] ≤ log p(D)
