# Stochastic Processes — Markov Chains and Beyond

## Markov Chains

A discrete-time Markov chain {X_n} satisfies the Markov property:

    P(X_{n+1} = j | X_n = i, X_{n-1}, ..., X₀) = P(X_{n+1} = j | X_n = i)

The transition matrix P = [p_{ij}] where p_{ij} = P(X_{n+1} = j | X_n = i).

## Stationary Distribution

π is a stationary distribution if π = π P. For an irreducible, aperiodic Markov
chain, π is unique and X_n →^D π regardless of the initial distribution.

## Poisson Process

A counting process N(t) is a Poisson process with rate λ if:
1. N(0) = 0
2. Independent increments
3. P(N(t+h) - N(t) = 1) = λh + o(h)
4. P(N(t+h) - N(t) ≥ 2) = o(h)

The inter-arrival times are i.i.d. Exponential(λ).

## Brownian Motion (Wiener Process)

A process W(t) is standard Brownian motion if:
1. W(0) = 0
2. Independent increments: W(t) - W(s) ~ N(0, t-s) for t > s
3. Continuous sample paths

The stochastic integral ∫ f(t) dW(t) is the foundation of stochastic calculus.
