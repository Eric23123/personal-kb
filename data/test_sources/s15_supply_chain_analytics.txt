# Supply Chain Analytics — Models and Metrics

## Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)

The classic EOQ model balances ordering costs and holding costs:

    TC = (D/Q) · S + (Q/2) · H + D · C

where D = annual demand, Q = order quantity, S = setup/ordering cost per order,
H = holding cost per unit per year, C = unit cost. The optimal order quantity:

    Q* = √(2DS / H)

Total relevant cost at Q*: TC* = √(2DSH). The reorder point ROP = d · L,
where d is daily demand and L is lead time in days.

## Newsvendor Model

For single-period stochastic demand with distribution F(d), the optimal order
quantity Q* satisfies the critical fractile:

    F(Q*) = c_u / (c_u + c_o)

where c_u = underage cost (lost profit per unit short) and c_o = overage cost
(loss per unit unsold). This is a cornerstone of perishable and seasonal inventory.

## Bullwhip Effect

The bullwhip effect describes increasing demand variability moving upstream in
a supply chain. Even small fluctuations in consumer demand can cause large swings
in orders at the manufacturer level. Key causes:

- Demand forecast updating
- Order batching
- Price fluctuations (forward buying)
- Rationing and shortage gaming

Quantification: Var(Orders) / Var(Demand) increases at each echelon. Information
sharing and VMI (Vendor-Managed Inventory) mitigate this effect.

## Facility Location and Network Design

The uncapacitated facility location problem (UFLP) minimizes:

    minimize Σ_j f_j y_j + Σ_i Σ_j c_{ij} x_{ij}
    subject to Σ_j x_{ij} = 1 ∀i,  x_{ij} ≤ y_j ∀i,j,  y_j ∈ {0,1}

where f_j is the fixed cost of opening facility j, c_{ij} is the transportation
cost from j to demand i. This is a mixed-integer linear program — see s11 for
LP fundamentals and s12 for branch-and-bound solution methods.

## Safety Stock and Service Levels

For normally distributed lead-time demand with mean μ_L and standard deviation
σ_L, the safety stock at service level α (cycle service level) is:

    SS = z_α · σ_L

where z_α = Φ⁻¹(α). For a (Q, R) continuous review policy with backordering,
the fill rate (fraction of demand filled from stock) is:

    Fill Rate = 1 - σ_L · L(z) / Q

where L(z) is the standard normal loss function: L(z) = φ(z) - z(1-Φ(z)).
